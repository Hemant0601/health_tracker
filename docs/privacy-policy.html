Health Tracker — Privacy Policy

App: Health Tracker (Android, package com.hemant.healthtracker) · Effective 17 November 2026

Health Tracker lets you record health readings — blood pressure, blood sugar, heart rate, oxygen (SpO₂), weight and temperature — for yourself and family members, see trends, and set reminders. This policy explains what happens to that information.

The short version

  • Everything you enter is stored only on your phone.
  • The app has no account, no servers, no analytics and no ads. We don't collect, receive, sell or share any of your data.
  • Uninstalling the app deletes all of its data from your phone.

Information you enter

Family member profiles (name, relation, gender, optional date of birth and height) and the readings you log, with their date, time and any note you add. This stays in the app's private storage on your device. We have no copy and no way to see it.

Reminders

Reminders are scheduled on your phone using Android's local notifications. No push service or server is involved.

Export and import

You can export your readings to a CSV file and share it using your phone's share sheet, or import a CSV file you choose. These only happen when you tap Export or Import, and the file goes only where you send it. Treat exported files as private health information.

Permissions

  • Notifications — to show the reminders you set.
  • Run at startup — to keep your reminders scheduled after the phone restarts.

The app does not request your location, contacts, camera, microphone, SMS or files beyond those you pick for import.

Deleting your data

Delete a reading or a member inside the app, or uninstall the app to remove everything. Because we never receive your data, there is nothing for us to delete on our side.

Children

The app is meant for adults. You can keep records for family members of any age; that information stays on your phone like everything else.

Not medical advice

Health Tracker is for personal record-keeping. Its normal and out-of-range labels are general guides, not a diagnosis. Talk to your doctor about your readings, and get medical help straight away in an emergency.
